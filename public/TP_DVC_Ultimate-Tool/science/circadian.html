Circadian rhythms¶

The DVC records activity continuously, 24/7, which makes it a natural instrument for chronobiology — the study of daily (circadian) rhythms. This page covers the full circadian battery the tool provides, from the classic cosinor fit to the distribution-free actigraphy descriptors and period estimation for free-running designs.

The reference validation

The DVC’s fitness for circadian phenotyping was established by Tir, Foster & Peirson (2025), Scientific Reports — showing DVC activity recovers the expected ~24 h period under a light/dark cycle, free-running under constant darkness, and detects arrhythmicity in clock-mutant (Cry1/Cry2) mice. Every circadian analysis here is grounded in that work. DOI: 10.1038/s41598-025-87530-6.

Zeitgeber time and the light/dark cycle¶

All circadian analyses are expressed in Zeitgeber time (ZT), the animal’s internal clock reference: ZT0 = lights-on, ZT12 = lights-off for a standard 12:12 cycle. The tool derives ZT from each timestamp using your configured light schedule (default lights-on 07:00, lights-off 19:00) and handles non-12:12 photoperiods and midnight-crossing dark phases correctly.

Cosinor rhythm analysis¶

What it tells you. The cosinor is the classic model of a daily rhythm: it fits a cosine wave to the activity across the day and reports three interpretable numbers — the rhythm-adjusted mean level, how large the daily swing is, and when the peak occurs. Crucially, this tool also tests whether a rhythm exists at all and puts confidence intervals on the estimates, so a flat, noisy trace can’t masquerade as a real rhythm.

The model. For each group and metric it fits

value = MESOR + amplitude · cos( 2π · (ZT − acrophase) / period )

with a default period of 24 h. It uses non-linear least squares (SciPy) when available, otherwise a closed-form linear cosinor.

Output

Meaning

MESOR

The rhythm-adjusted mean (the midline of the fitted cosine — a better “average activity” than a raw mean because it accounts for the rhythm).

Amplitude

Half the peak-to-trough swing — the strength of the daily rhythm.

Acrophase (acrophase_ZT)

The ZT hour at which activity peaks — the timing of the rhythm.

p_rhythm

An F-test that the amplitude is non-zero, i.e. that a rhythm is actually present. This is the standard cosinor rhythm-detection test.

Confidence intervals

95% CIs for amplitude and acrophase (amplitude_ci_low/high, acrophase_ci_low/high), derived by the delta method. Acrophase CIs can wrap around midnight — flagged by acrophase_ci_wraps.

R²

Goodness of fit of the cosine.

Honest confidence intervals

By default the fit is done at the subject level (per-animal ZT-bin means), which gives honest residual degrees of freedom and meaningful confidence intervals — rather than pooling all raw points, which would understate uncertainty.

Tool: summarize_circadian_cosinor.

Non-parametric circadian descriptors (IS / IV / RA, M10 / L5)¶

Why not just cosinor? Rodent home-cage activity is not a smooth sinusoid — it has sharp dark-onset bursts and fragmented rest. The field-standard, distribution-free descriptors (from human/animal actigraphy, the van Someren family) capture that structure directly and are directly comparable to the actigraphy literature.

Metric

What it measures

IS — interdaily stability

How reproducible the 24 h pattern is from one day to the next. Range 0–1; higher = more stable. Computed as the between-day variance of the average daily profile divided by the overall variance.

IV — intradaily variability

How fragmented the rest/activity rhythm is (frequent switching between rest and activity). Higher = more fragmented. Computed from the mean squared hour-to-hour difference over the overall variance.

M10

Mean activity during the most-active 10 hours of the day.

L5

Mean activity during the least-active 5 hours of the day.

RA — relative amplitude

(M10 − L5) / (M10 + L5) — a robust, normalised measure of rhythm strength that doesn’t assume a sinusoid.

These are often the single highest-value circadian readout for genotype or treatment effects — a reduced IS or elevated IV can reveal a phenotype that mean activity misses entirely.

Tool: summarize_nonparametric_circadian.

Activity bouts & fragmentation¶

What it tells you. Effects often live in the structure of activity — how it’s broken into bouts — even when the 24 h mean is unchanged. This analysis classifies each time bin as active or inactive (threshold auto-set to the per-animal median, or a value you choose) and summarises the bout structure.

Outputs include: number of active bouts, mean and maximum active-bout duration (minutes), the same for inactive bouts, total active minutes, fraction of time active, and the mean daily total activity.

Tool: summarize_activity_bouts.

Light / dark analysis¶

What it tells you. The simplest circadian readout: how much activity happens in the dark (active) phase versus the light (rest) phase, and the dark/light ratio — a basic diurnality index. Means are computed over per-subject phase means to avoid pseudo-replication.

Tool: summarize_light_dark. Grounding: Tir et al. 2025; Tomanelli et al. 2024 (10.3389/fonc.2024.1504938).

Period estimation & periodograms¶

For free-running designs (constant darkness) or tau-mutant lines, the period is not fixed at 24 h and must be estimated from the data. The tool offers two established periodogram methods, both robust to the irregular sampling left by exclusions and daily welfare checks.

Method

Notes

Lomb–Scargle periodogram

Estimates the dominant period by scanning candidate periods (default 18–30 h) and finding the strongest spectral peak. Handles uneven sampling natively. Returns the estimated period and peak power.

Chi-square (Sokolove–Bushell / Qp) periodogram

The standard chronobiology periodogram (as in ActogramJ). For each trial period the data are folded and the Qp statistic — the ratio of between-phase to total variance — is tested against a chi-square significance threshold, giving a per-period p-value and a clear “is this period significant?” line.

Tools: estimate_period (Lomb–Scargle), compute_periodogram and compute_chi_square_periodogram (chrono viz).

Actograms & activity onsets¶

Actogram. The classic visualization of circadian data: a double-plotted raster with successive days stacked vertically and each day shown twice side-by-side, so drifting rhythms read as a diagonal band. This is the first plot any chronobiologist looks at.

Activity onsets. For each day, the tool detects the onset (first time activity rises above the day’s mean) and the acrophase (the day’s peak time) — the day-by-day timing that reveals entrainment, phase shifts and jet-lag re-entrainment.

Tools: prepare_actogram_matrix / plot_actogram, detect_activity_onsets.

Group-housed recordings¶

DVC signals are cage-level (not per-animal). Reassuringly, DVC circadian profiles from group-housed cages have been shown to be robust, reproducible and comparable to conventional individual measurement — enabling welfare-friendly, high-throughput longitudinal designs.

Grounding: Sun et al. 2024 (10.3389/fnins.2024.1456307).
