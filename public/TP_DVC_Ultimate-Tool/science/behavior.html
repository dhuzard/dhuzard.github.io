Sleep & behaviour¶

Continuous activity data lets you estimate sleep without any extra equipment, and — with the multivariate tools — summarise an animal’s whole behavioural phenotype in a way you can compare across cages and days.

Immobility-defined sleep¶

What it tells you. In rodents, sustained immobility is a well-established proxy for sleep. The tool scores a time bin as inactive when activity falls to (or below) a threshold, and marks a run of consecutive inactive bins as sleep only when it lasts at least a minimum duration — the widely used 40-second immobility-defined-sleep criterion.

From the scored trace it derives a full sleep architecture summary:

Metric

Meaning

Total sleep & sleep %

Total sleep minutes and the fraction of the recording spent asleep.

Bout counts & durations

Number of sleep bouts, mean / median / longest bout (minutes).

Fragmentation index

Sleep bouts per hour of sleep — higher means more broken-up sleep.

Light vs dark sleep

With a light schedule, sleep % in each phase and the dark/light sleep ratio.

Sleep probability by hour

Average probability of sleep at each hour of day — the daily sleep profile.

Use a fine time bin

Sleep scoring needs sub-minute resolution to be meaningful. If the bin size is as large as the 40 s criterion, every immobile bin trivially counts as “sleep” and the architecture metrics collapse — the tool warns you when this happens. The DVC supports 250 ms and 1-minute native recording, which is ideal here. The tool also refuses to upsample past the native resolution (which would fabricate inactive bins and inflate sleep).

Tools: score_sleep_wake, summarize_sleep_metrics, sleep_probability_by_hour. Grounding: Tir et al. 2025 (10.1038/s41598-025-87530-6), which notes immobility correlates with sleep bouts at this resolution; excessive rest during the active phase as a disease signal is from Golini et al. 2023 (10.3389/fnbeh.2023.1130055).

Multivariate phenotyping (PCA)¶

What it tells you. Instead of reading a dozen metrics one at a time, principal component analysis (PCA) compresses an animal’s whole behavioural profile into a 2-D map, so cages and treatment groups that behave differently separate visually — and you can see which features drive the difference.

The unit of observation is one cage × experiment day. For each, the tool computes a feature vector:

Feature

Meaning

mean_activity

Overall activity level.

dark_fraction

Share of activity in the dark phase.

amplitude

Peak-to-trough of the ZT-binned daily profile.

m10, l5, relative_amplitude

Most-active-10 h / least-active-5 h and their relative amplitude (see Circadian rhythms).

iv

Intradaily variability (fragmentation).

peak_sin, peak_cos

The acrophase encoded circularly, so 23:30 and 00:30 sit next to each other (a naive “hour” would place them 24 apart).

PCA is computed by singular value decomposition on the standardized features — no machine-learning dependency. You can:

  • view a scree plot (how much variance each component explains),

  • view a scores scatter (each cage-day as a point; optionally connect a cage’s days to show drift over time),

  • view loadings (which features define each axis),

  • or run a functional PCA on the full time-of-day profile, where the loadings are shown as curves over the day — PC1 typically captures overall amplitude, PC2 an early-versus-late phase/shape contrast.

Per-cage-day features can be aggregated to one row per subject (averaging across days, with a proper circular mean for acrophase) to give a single typical phenotype per animal without pseudo-replication.

Tools: compute_cage_day_features, aggregate_subject_features, compute_subject_profile_matrix, compute_pca, and the plot_pca_* / plot_profile_loadings figures.
